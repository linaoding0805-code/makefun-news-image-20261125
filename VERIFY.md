## 2. Lead — FCC authorises SpaceX to deploy up to 1,000,000 satellites for Starmind

Page text: *"The FCC authorises SpaceX to deploy up to 1,000,000 satellites for Starmind, the first orbital AI data-centre network in history — first satellite by 2033, the full fleet by 2040."*

**Verdict: PARTIAL**

What checks out:

- **Number (1,000,000) and name (Starmind): correct.** Reuters, 25 Nov 2026: "The Federal Communications Commission on Wednesday said it was authorizing Elon Musk's SpaceX to deploy 1 million satellites for data centers in space… the FCC said it was approving the SpaceX 'Starmind' constellation." WSJ, 25 Nov 2026: "SpaceX has won approval from the Federal Communications Commission to launch one million data centers into space… known as Starmind."
- **2033 / 2040 milestones: correct as regulatory deadlines.** WSJ, 25 Nov 2026: "The FCC authorization gives SpaceX until 2033 to launch and operate the first satellite and until 2040 to launch the full constellation." Spaceflight Now, 26 Nov 2026 (reporting the 25 Nov FCC order): first satellite by 25 Nov 2033, 50% by 25 Nov 2038, full constellation by 25 Nov 2040.
- **It is a first — but only as worded by the FCC.** The FCC called it "the first authorization in U.S. history for a constellation of data centers in space" (reported by USA Today, 25 Nov 2026; GeekWire, 25 Nov 2026). The front page drops both qualifiers ("U.S." and "authorization") and upgrades it to "the first orbital AI data-centre network in history". No such network exists yet — nothing has launched — and other companies (Google, Blue Origin) are pursuing the same goal (Gizmodo, 25 Nov 2026). The page's phrasing presents an authorisation as if it were a built network, and a US first as if it were a world first.

**Corrected wording:** *"The FCC authorises SpaceX to deploy up to 1,000,000 satellites for Starmind — the first US authorisation of an orbital AI data-centre constellation — with the first satellite due by 2033 and the full fleet by 2040."* (Note also that SpaceX itself says it plans to launch the first Starmind satellites in 2027 — WSJ, 25 Nov 2026 — so "by 2033" is the FCC deadline, not SpaceX's plan.)

- Sources: Reuters, 25 Nov 2026; WSJ, 25 Nov 2026; USA Today, 25 Nov 2026; GeekWire, 25 Nov 2026; Spaceflight Now, 26 Nov 2026.

## 3. "US TARIFFS HIT 14 NATIONS"

Page text: *"Washington proposes new duties after an eight-month probe: China 20%, the EU 15%, others 10–20%. Hearings in January; the earliest start is January."*

**Verdict: PARTIAL**

What checks out:

- **14 partners, eight-month probe, announced Wednesday 25 Nov 2026: correct.** Reuters, 25 Nov 2026 (read in full): "The Trump administration on Wednesday proposed new tariffs on 14 major trading partners… The US Trade Representative's office announced the duties after completing an eight-month Section 301 unfair trade practices probe into 16 trading partners." The 14 named: China, Bangladesh, Cambodia, EU, India, Indonesia, Japan, South Korea, Malaysia, Singapore, Switzerland, Taiwan, Thailand, Vietnam. Mexico and Norway were excluded (probes ongoing).
- **China 20%, EU 15%: correct as the capped rates.** Reuters, 25 Nov 2026: China "would face the highest tariff rates… the proposed 20% excess-capacity tariffs"; the 15% cap agreed by the EU, Japan, South Korea and Taiwan. CNN, 25 Nov 2026: "At 20%, China is set to face the highest proposed tariff rate… The European Union's proposed starting rate would be 15%."
- **Hearings in January; earliest start January: correct.** Kitco (Reuters), 25 Nov 2026: USTR Jamieson Greer said the tariffs "would not take effect until after a January 25 public hearing"; CNN, 25 Nov 2026: "The earliest the administration expects the tariffs to take effect is January." (Written comments due 5 Jan 2027; hearings begin 25 Jan 2027 — Global Trade & Sanctions Law, 25 Nov 2026.)

What needs correcting:

- **"Others 10–20%" is true only of the capped rates, and omits the conditional higher rates.** The capped range does run 10% (Singapore) to 20% (China; Vietnam capped at 20% if a deal is reached), with 15% for the EU/Japan/Korea/Taiwan/Switzerland, 17% for India, 18% for Cambodia/Indonesia/Malaysia/Thailand/Bangladesh. But the headline proposals run 20–40% (Kitco/Reuters, 25 Nov 2026), and the caps rise if trade deals fail: China to 40% if the truce unravels, Vietnam to 35%, India to 25% (Kitco/Reuters; CNN, 25 Nov 2026).
- **China's 20% stacks on top of existing duties.** Reuters, 25 Nov 2026: the 20% is "in addition to the roughly 25% tariffs imposed during President Donald Trump's first term". The page's bare "China 20%" understates the effective rate.

**Corrected wording:** *"Washington proposes new duties after an eight-month probe: China 20% and the EU 15% (capped rates; others 10–20%, but headline proposals run to 40% and rise if trade deals fail). Hearings begin 25 January; the earliest start is January."*

- Sources: Reuters, 25 Nov 2026 (read in full); CNN, 25 Nov 2026; Kitco (Reuters), 25 Nov 2026; Global Trade & Sanctions Law, 25 Nov 2026.

## 4. "KYIV HITS AIR BASE"

Page text: *"Ukraine says it struck Savasleyka air base, hitting three MiG-31s and two Su-34s. Drones also hit two refineries in Samara. Russia has not commented."*

**Verdict: PARTIAL**

What checks out:

- **Three MiG-31s and two Su-34s, attributed to Ukraine: correct as a claim.** BBC, 25 Nov 2026: "Ukraine has said it damaged five Russian military aircraft in an attack on Russia's Savasleyka airfield… Three MiG-31 and two Su-34s aircraft were hit, Kyiv's General Staff said, adding that the extent of the damage was still being assessed." Defence Blog, 25 Nov 2026 (read in full): Ukraine's General Staff said three MiG-31s and two Su-34s "were hit and 'will be unable to fly for some time,' citing preliminary information. 'The extent of the damage and the exact number of aircraft affected are still being verified.'"
- **Two refineries in Samara: correct as a claim.** BBC, 25 Nov 2026: "Overnight, Kyiv also said it hit two oil refineries in Russia's Samara region" (the Syzran and Kuibyshev refineries). Defence Blog, 25 Nov 2026: "Overnight, Ukraine also targeted the Syzran and Kuibyshev oil refineries in Russia's Samara region."
- **Damage figures are Ukraine's claim only: the page handles this correctly in the first sentence ("Ukraine says it struck…"), but the second and third sentences drop the attribution.** The figures are preliminary, the extent of damage is still being assessed, and no satellite imagery of damage at the base had surfaced by Wednesday afternoon (Defence Blog, 25 Nov 2026).

What needs correcting:

- **"Russia has not commented" is inaccurate.** Russia's Defence Ministry did comment on the night's events: it said its air defences shot down 305 Ukrainian drones over 13 regions overnight, including Nizhny Novgorod — it simply did not mention Savasleyka (Defence Blog, 25 Nov 2026). The correct statement is that Russia has not commented *on Savasleyka*.
- **Date note (see §8): the air-base strikes began on 24 November** and continued overnight into Wednesday 25 November (Defence Blog; BBC, 25 Nov 2026). The claim was announced on Wednesday morning, so it is legitimate 25 Nov news, but the strikes themselves straddle 24–25 Nov.

**Corrected wording:** *"Ukraine says it struck Savasleyka air base on 24–25 November, hitting three MiG-31s and two Su-34s — preliminary figures, damage still being assessed. Drones also hit two refineries in Samara, Kyiv says. Russia has not commented on Savasleyka, but says it shot down 305 drones overnight."*

- Sources: BBC, 25 Nov 2026; Defence Blog, 25 Nov 2026 (read in full); NY Post, 25 Nov 2026.

## 5. "PARADE OFF IN THE STORM"

Page text: *"Macy's Thanksgiving parade moves to Saturday as a nor'easter bears down on New York, with gusts of 60–65 mph forecast. Its first weather postponement ever."*

**Verdict: PASS (with one precision note)**

- **Postponed to Saturday: correct.** WSJ, 25 Nov 2026 (read in full): "A powerful nor'easter has forced Macy's to reschedule its Thanksgiving Day Parade… Officials made the decision to wait until Saturday"; the parade "would now kick off Saturday at 8:30 a.m." NY Post, 25 Nov 2026: organisers rescheduled "from Thursday to Saturday morning". The decision was announced Wednesday night, 25 Nov 2026 (Macy's executive Will Coss "said Wednesday night" — WSJ).
- **Gusts of 60–65 mph: correct.** WSJ, 25 Nov 2026: NYC Mayor Zohran Mamdani said the city expected "sustained winds between 30 and 40 mph, with possible gusts as high as 60 to 65 mph". USA Today, 25 Nov 2026: the National Weather Service high-wind warning cited gusts "potentially reaching 60 mph".
- **First weather postponement ever: correct as worded.** NY Post, 25 Nov 2026: "The decision marks the first time the parade has ever been postponed"; The Times, 25 Nov 2026: "postponed for the first time in its 100-year history". Precision note: the parade *has* been cancelled before — three times, in 1942, 1943 and 1944, because of a wartime rubber and helium shortage — and its balloons were grounded once by weather, in 1971 (63 mph gust in Central Park) (WSJ, 25 Nov 2026). The page's word "postponement" is the precise one and is accurate; "first weather postponement ever" stands, but it should not be shortened to "first cancellation" or "first time weather stopped the parade".

No correction required.

- Sources: WSJ, 25 Nov 2026 (read in full); NY Post, 25 Nov 2026; USA Today, 25 Nov 2026; The Times, 25 Nov 2026.

## 6. ALSO TODAY

### 6a. PCE 3.8%, core 3.3% — PASS on the numbers, PARTIAL on "a three-year high"

- **Numbers correct.** Barron's, 25 Nov 2026: "The personal consumption expenditures price index rose 3.8% in October from a year earlier… Core PCE… climbed to 3.3% from 3.1%." Reuters wrapup, 25 Nov 2026: "PCE inflation increased 3.8%… core PCE inflation increased 3.3% on a year-over-year basis in October." Both released by the Bureau of Economic Analysis on Wednesday 25 Nov 2026 at 08:30 ET.
- **"A three-year high" is imprecise as attached to both figures.** Headline PCE at 3.8% is the highest since May 2023 (Zacks, 25 Nov 2026) — more than three years — while core PCE at 3.3% is the largest advance since November 2023 (Reuters, 25 Nov 2026), described elsewhere as "its highest level in nearly three years" (TradingView/Cointelegraph, 25 Nov 2026). The phrase is defensible for the headline figure but overstates the core figure by a hair and is ambiguous about which series it modifies.

**Corrected wording:** *"Hot US prices: PCE inflation 3.8% — its highest since May 2023 — and core PCE 3.3%, its highest since November 2023."*

### 6b. Q3 growth revised to 4.3% — PASS

Barron's, 25 Nov 2026: "the economy grew at a 4.3% annualized rate in the third quarter, up from the 3.4% initially reported." Reuters wrapup, 25 Nov 2026: "GDP increased at a 4.3% annualized rate last quarter, the Commerce Department's Bureau of Economic Analysis said in its second estimate." No correction.

### 6c. Fed hike near-certain in December — PASS

### 6d. Iran says two tankers hit mines in Hormuz — PASS (as attributed)

Reuters, 25 Nov 2026 (read in full via KRRO reprint): "Two oil tankers struck naval mines, exploded and caught fire in the Strait of Hormuz on Wednesday, Iran's Revolutionary Guards Navy said in a statement carried by state media." The page correctly attributes this to Iran ("Iran says"). Flag for the record: this is a single-source claim — the IRGC statement via IRNA named no ship, no flag and no crew, and no independent/maritime-agency confirmation had appeared by Wednesday afternoon (TML News, 25 Nov 2026). The page's attribution makes the wording acceptable; it must not be upgraded to unqualified fact.

### 6e. Lukoil's overseas assets "sold in a $10bn deal" — PARTIAL (overstates completion)

- **The $10bn figure and the 25 Nov date are correct**, but this was a signed agreement, not a completed sale. Reuters, 25 Nov 2026 (read in full): Boehly "said on Wednesday he had signed a deal with Lukoil… The deal… involves swapping Lukoil securities worth $10 billion for the company's international assets." WSJ, 25 Nov 2026, likewise: "The consortium said no cash is exchanging hands… The agreement is still subject to approval from the U.S. Treasury Department's Office of Foreign Assets Control." Previous bidders (Gunvor/Centalion, Carlyle) signed deals that never closed.

**Corrected wording:** *"Lukoil's overseas assets in a $10bn share-swap deal — signed, pending US Treasury approval, closing up to a year away."*

- Sources: Barron's, 25 Nov 2026; Reuters, 25 Nov 2026; Zacks, 25 Nov 2026; Investor's Business Daily, 25 Nov 2026; FXStreet, 25 Nov 2026; WSJ, 25 Nov 2026; TradingView, 25 Nov 2026; TML News, 25 Nov 2026.

2. **Tariffs (§3):** "others 10–20%" → capped rates of 10–20%, with headline proposals to 40% and higher rates if trade deals fail (China 40%, Vietnam 35%, India 25%); note China's 20% stacks on ~25% first-term duties.
3. **Kyiv (§4):** date the strikes 24–25 November; keep the damage figures explicitly attributed to Ukraine as preliminary; replace "Russia has not commented" with "Russia has not commented on Savasleyka, but says it shot down 305 drones overnight".
4. **Also Today — PCE (§6a):** replace the blanket "a three-year high" with "headline PCE 3.8%, its highest since May 2023; core PCE 3.3%, its highest since November 2023".
5. **Also Today — Lukoil (§6e):** "sold in a $10bn deal" → "in a $10bn share-swap deal — signed, pending US Treasury (OFAC) approval, closing up to a year away; no cash changes hands".
6. **Caption (§8):** "Hundreds of satellites" → drop the count or say "Thousands", to match the illustration.

No claim was found to be unverifiable, and no correction above changes the substance of any story — but corrections 1–5 should be made before the page is submitted as a factual front page.
